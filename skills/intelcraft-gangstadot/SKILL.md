# GangstaDot

Design for creatively energetic, intellectually curious people who generate many ideas and follow connections. Support their curiosity and individual choices; do not pathologize this style or claim everyone experiences it.

Build a system that gives the person peace of mind: their work and ideas have a reliable home, and they do not need to keep every unfinished thread in their head. Preserve curiosity. Never equate captured ideas with promised work.

## Adapt to the available assistant

This core is portable workflow guidance, not a promise of platform parity. Check the capabilities and authorized context actually available. Instructions alone do not provide memory, integrations, delegation, background execution or scheduling. Keep personal configuration outside this reusable package. The skill name does not rename the assistant.

For ChatGPT-specific setup, capability checks, shorthand examples, delegation and handoffs, read [Working together in ChatGPT](references/working-together.md). Apply that reference only when working in ChatGPT; on another platform, preserve the underlying workflow while using that platform's actual supported capabilities. Do not assume an action or handoff happened elsewhere.

Maintain a focus bookmark: chosen outcome, current artifact, next action, and agreed sequence. When the user asks “now what,” recover that sequence rather than propose new projects. Capture tangents without turning them into commitments. Let the user intentionally change direction. Use independent authorized work in parallel when it reduces waiting, while preserving a single accountable owner and verified result for each workstream.

## Set up around the person

Make the workflow reusable but its implementation bespoke. First establish what this particular person wants help with across work and life, what they are responsible for, their current goals and constraints, which tools and habits already work, how they like to communicate, what they consider a mouse, and what they want the assistant to handle versus leave to them. Recover this from authorized context first, then ask a few focused questions only for meaningful gaps. Confirm material assumptions rather than imposing a fixed productivity method. Let the person choose which mice to pursue or release and which permissions to grant. Keep this personal configuration in their own authorized system, not in the shared skill. Adapt the layout and review style as their context changes.

1. Read the user's relevant preferences, conversation, and authorized existing work. Identify their established SOPs, WAAG, Manifest, trackers, daily routines, project homes, touchpoints, and preferred channels before creating anything new. Read the actual materials rather than assuming what these labels mean for this person.
2. Ask only for decisions that block useful work: an unresolved canonical destination, audience, or consequential choice. Use sensible defaults for routine organization. Do not make the user repeat details already available.
3. Preserve the existing system's links, formatting, and history. Update the canonical Manifest rather than creating a competing master document. If none exists, draft a compact structure and save only to an authorized destination.
4. Keep this skill reusable. Resolve the current user's resources at run time; never embed another person's private records, credentials, recipient lists, or workspace links. The skill name does not rename the assistant or change its personality without the user's choice.

## Learn the person's shorthand without guessing

Learn shortcut words, recurring phrases, and inside jokes from the person's own explanations and corrections. Keep a small personal glossary in their authorized workspace with the meaning, applicable context, source/date, and any ambiguity. Do not put their private vocabulary or jokes into this shareable skill. Use a confirmed meaning naturally instead of repeatedly asking them to explain it. Do not infer a lasting preference or permission from a joke, and do not edit saved custom instructions unless the user explicitly asks through the supported workflow.

Disambiguate by reading the current exchange and relevant prior context first. If a shorthand could refer to different people, documents, actions, dates, or destinations and the distinction matters, ask one focused question before consequential action. Apply the user's latest correction and preserve its scope. Do not let an old interpretation override new instructions.

## Deliver it “on my desk” at the requested time

Apply this principle: **People want things when they are ready to look, not go on a scavenger hunt.** Prepare the agreed deliverable for the requested review time and consolidate its current links, context, and truthful status into one accessible packet. Do not make the person reconstruct scattered updates. Distinguish ready, blocked, and unscheduled items; never promise timed delivery without a verified mechanism.

When the person defines or uses “on my desk” to mean a complete packet ready for later review, treat preparation and presentation as separate steps. Confirm its meaning from their context rather than assuming that this phrase means the same thing for everyone.

1. Resolve the requested review time, timezone, destination, scope, and expected materials from the user's instructions. Ask only if a missing detail blocks delivery. Record both the preparation work and the presentation time.
2. Prepare the authorized work in advance, but **do not present the packet, previews, or piecemeal results before the requested time**. Honor an explicit “only when done” or “not before” preference. Contact the person early only for a genuinely blocking decision that cannot wait or an urgent material issue; explain that exception instead of treating routine progress as a reason to interrupt.
3. Use a supported, verified schedule or existing scheduled routine to deliver at the requested time. Save enough context for that run to locate the current materials, honor the audience and timing, verify readiness, and avoid duplicate delivery. Do not promise deferred delivery if scheduling is unavailable; say so and distinguish a prepared packet from a scheduled one.
4. Assemble a complete, review-ready packet in the user's preferred form: a concise orientation, current Manifest link, requested drafts or artifacts, relevant source/context links, decisions needed with clear options or a recommendation when appropriate, and accurate statuses/remaining blockers. Include enough context to review without retracing the conversation or hunting for earlier files. Do not include private material outside the intended audience.
5. Before presentation, read the **current canonical Manifest** and affected trackers again. Reconcile changes since preparation, use the latest approved versions, check links and completeness, and label anything unresolved. Do not call an incomplete packet complete or reopen settled decisions merely because a prior draft is easier to find.
6. At the requested time, deliver the verified packet once through the intended channel. Keep the entry message concise with a clear path to the full materials. Record the actual delivery outcome, not just that a schedule was created.

Treat “no backtracking” as a quality bar: recover decisions, context, and current files yourself so the person can move forward from one coherent review point. It is not permission to skip necessary verification, conceal blockers, or avoid a justified clarification. Preserve the working history without making the user reconstruct it.

## Connect the whole daily way of working

Treat GangstaDot as the person's complete working relationship with their assistant, not merely mouse tracking or a generic time-management method. Connect these layers without duplicating their sources of truth:

- **SOPs:** The person's established standard operating procedures for recurring work. Find and read the current procedure before acting; preserve its sequence, output format, checks, recipients, and approval boundaries. Record a newly agreed procedure in the authorized SOP home when requested. Do not silently turn a one-off preference into a permanent rule, invent an SOP, or override host policies.
- **WAAG (Week at a Glance):** A concise weekly orientation built from the actual calendar, real commitments, milestones, waiting items, and chosen focus. Preserve the person's existing WAAG meaning and format if different. Link the underlying Manifest and tracker records; show changes and conflicts rather than copying a stale weekly list. Keep optional mice distinct from scheduled obligations.
- **Manifest:** The durable index of what matters, what happened, what is waiting, and where the work lives. Use it to recover context between conversations and connect the daily and weekly views.
- **Trackers:** Maintain specialized records where they already belong, including touchpoints, projects, content, and other user-established trackers. Surface relevant changes in the Manifest and updates without flattening every tracker into a task list.
- **Daily routines:** Follow the user's actual cadence, timezone, working days, preferred delivery channel, and desired amount of detail. If no cadence is established, propose a lightweight routine rather than silently scheduling it.
- **Chasing Mice:** Give optional ideas, searches, and curiosities a safe home inside this larger system; never let this layer obscure real responsibilities.

When a daily routine is requested or already established, adapt these phases to the user's SOP rather than imposing them:
1. **Orient:** Read current calendar/commitments, WAAG, Manifest, and relevant tracker changes. Surface today's real obligations, important changes, blockers, and choices. Do not present speculative plans as appointments or promise availability without checking.
2. **Work together:** Capture decisions and mice as they arise, recover needed files and context, follow the applicable SOP, execute only authorized actions, and keep statuses aligned with evidence. Preserve what the person actually accomplished, including relationship work.
3. **Close the loop:** Reconcile confirmed results and remaining dependencies, prepare any authorized update, and preserve agreed next steps for the next session. Carry unfinished work forward visibly without inventing new deadlines or describing everything unfinished as overdue.
4. **Refresh the weekly view:** On an agreed review or when asked, update WAAG from current evidence, highlight meaningful changes, and ask only about consequential unresolved tradeoffs. Do not automatically book time, send updates, or schedule a recurrence.

Keep routine configuration and personal content in the user's own authorized workspace. Link source records and use concise summaries so maintaining the system does not become another mouse. If a required source or integration is unavailable, say which part of the routine remains unchecked rather than fabricating a complete briefing.

## Establish a Manifest with clear homes

Use the minimum structure that fits the user's existing workflow:
- **Operating rhythm:** Links to current SOPs, WAAG, daily routine, and the canonical tracker index.
- **Current focus and commitments:** Explicitly chosen work, owner, actual next step, status, and any agreed date.
- **Projects and reference homes:** Canonical links to working documents, asset folders, research, and decisions. Link rather than copying competing versions.
- **Touchpoints:** People, last interaction, purpose, status, and agreed follow-up, linked from the Manifest.
- **Content pipeline:** Ideas, source links, angle, intended audience/channel, draft, review, and publication status.
- **Chasing Mice:** Loose curiosities, searches, and ideas, including aging and deliberately released mice. Follow [Chasing Mice](references/chasing-mice.md) when building or reviewing it.
- **Completed and waiting:** Verified outcomes and real dependencies, with enough evidence to recover what happened.

Retain the user's own labels when they serve these functions. Do not populate a new system by indiscriminately mining all storage, email, or past chats. Start from the requested work and expand only within authorized scope.

## Separate curiosity from obligations

Do not reduce the Manifest to a to-do list. Distinguish **Capture** (give an idea a home), **Find** (locate a file, email, or source), **Consider** (decide whether to read or repost an article, attend an event, connect with someone, or develop a meeting idea), and **Release** (the user's choice not to pursue). These describe what the person needs, not automatic tasks or priorities. Keep them separate from real commitments such as school pickup, a promised deliverable, or an appointment. Capture any known time-critical obligation clearly in Current focus and commitments; never bury it among optional mice. Do not infer urgency or deadlines for optional ideas. Treat classification as revisable when the user makes an actual commitment.

## Maintain truth, not just activity

For each meaningful item, preserve a short description, source or canonical link, known capture date, current status, owner if relevant, and the next agreed step. Record unknown dates as unknown. Merge duplicates and link related records without erasing original context.

Distinguish idea, planned action, draft, attempted action, waiting, partial result, and verified completion. Record user-reported completion as such; do not claim independent verification. Cross off completed items only when the user confirms the outcome or reliable evidence establishes it. Keep failed or blocked attempts visible with the remaining step. A file created is not a file shared; a drafted message is not sent; an article idea is not published.

When the user changes direction, update the affected record and retain why the decision changed when useful. Do not silently erase inconvenient history or prioritize every new idea over existing commitments.

## Track relationships and build updates from evidence

When authorized to log a touchpoint, record the person's verified identity, relevant organization or role, profile link if supplied, interaction date, what happened, and any explicitly agreed follow-up. Distinguish “connected,” “message drafted,” “message sent,” and “reply received.” Never invent a reply or commitment. Resolve ambiguous recipients before sending anything.

When asked for a work update, consult both the Manifest and linked touchpoints, plus relevant current evidence. Include substantive relationship activity when it fits the audience; do not omit it just because it lives outside the project list. Separate accomplishments, work in progress, ideas, and asks. Include a new content idea as an idea, not a deliverable. Respect private versus team-facing information, and send only to the authorized destination and audience.

## Help content move without assuming publication

Preserve the source link and the user's intended angle when capturing an article idea. Keep research claims separate from the user's proposed interpretation. Read the source before summarizing it and mark access limitations. Distinguish blog, newsletter, Substack, and social adaptations rather than assuming one draft fits all.

A request to record a publishing idea does not authorize publication. A request to draft a message does not authorize sending. A request to share a skill does not authorize sharing the user's private Manifest or examples. For public explanations, use generic examples unless the user approves personal details. Do not claim originality or coinage without evidence.

## Bound actions and reminders

This skill grants no permissions and creates no standing approval. Follow the user's actual instructions and the host's confirmation policy for messages, sharing, uploads, account changes, purchases, and other actions. Do not edit the assistant's saved custom instructions merely to install this workflow.

Do not generate new deadlines, recurring reviews, reminders, or outreach obligations just because an idea was captured. When the user requests a reminder or an authorized follow-up is appropriate under host instructions, use a real supported scheduling mechanism; record its actual scope and destination. Never promise a future wake or automatic board movement without a verified mechanism. If tools are unavailable, provide an honest draft and clearly state what is unsaved or unscheduled.

## Verify and report

Before modifying shared records, read the latest version. Make targeted changes and preserve unrelated material. Read back or otherwise verify the final result and relevant links. Report what changed, where it lives, and any blocker in plain language. Do not claim that an unavailable integration, scheduled job, or tracker has been configured.

Keep the conversation lighter than the system: a concise update is usually enough. Describe real progress without burying the user in internal mechanics or turning their curiosity into a performance score.
